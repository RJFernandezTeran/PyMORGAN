\section{Two-dimensional data (2D-IR, 2D-ES, 2D-EV, and 2D-VE)}\label{sec:twoD}

The two-dimensional branch follows the same \emph{load $\to$ process $\to$
plot $\to$ analyse} pipeline as the one-dimensional one (chapter~\ref{sec:oneD}),
built around a single \code{Dataset2D} object. It is designed for general two-dimensional optical correlation spectroscopy across all spectral regimes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{2D-IR} (Two-Dimensional Infrared Spectroscopy: vibrational pump $\omega_1$ and vibrational probe $\omega_3$ in the mid-IR).
  \item \textbf{2D-ES} (Two-Dimensional Electronic Spectroscopy: electronic pump $\omega_1$ and electronic probe $\omega_3$ in the UV/Vis/NIR).
  \item \textbf{2D-EV} (Two-Dimensional Electronic--Vibrational Spectroscopy: electronic excitation $\omega_1$ in the UV/Vis coupled to vibrational detection $\omega_3$ in the mid-IR).
  \item \textbf{2D-VE} (Two-Dimensional Vibrational--Electronic Spectroscopy: vibrational excitation $\omega_1$ in the mid-IR coupled to electronic detection $\omega_3$ in the UV/Vis/NIR).
\end{itemize}
Because \code{Dataset2D} stores the pump ($\omega_1$) and probe ($\omega_3$) axes independently, both homonuclear (2D-IR, 2D-ES) and cross-regime heteronuclear (2D-EV, 2D-VE) measurements are handled uniformly. Table~\ref{tab:stages2d}
maps each stage to its module.

\subsection{Data model}
A two-dimensional measurement is a stack of 2-D frequency--frequency maps, one
per population time $t_2$. The signal is stored as a cube
\begin{equation}
  Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\text{pump}} \times N_{\text{probe}} \times N_{t_2}},
\end{equation}
indexed by the pump frequency $\omega_1$, the probe frequency $\omega_3$ and the
population time $t_2$. When single-scan loading (\code{load\_single\_scans=True}) or slow-modulation state datasets are present, \code{Dataset2D} also stores the per-scan and per-state tensors. The fields held by \code{Dataset2D} are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:fields2d}.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering

    \code{probe}           & $N_{\text{probe}}$ & probe axis $\omega_3$ \\

\subsection{Loading and the loader registry}
As in 1-D, formats are resolved through a registry
(\code{pymorgan.twoD.load}). The registered readers are \code{P2DAT} (processed
maps), \code{MESS\_2DIR}, \code{PyMESS\_2D} (alias \code{PyMESS\_2DIR}), \code{UoS\_2DIR}, \code{RAL\_RAW} (raw
interferograms, phased and transformed on load) and \code{RAL\_Proc};
\code{pm.available\_map\_loaders()} lists what the running installation
recognises.

import pymorgan as pm

data = pm.load_2D("scan.p2dat", data_type="P2DAT")

\paragraph{The P2DAT format.}
The first row is \code{0, 0, $t_2^{(1)}$, $t_2^{(2)}$, \dots} (the population
times); each subsequent row is \code{pump, probe, S($t_2^{(1)}$), \dots}. The
flat pump/probe ordering is reshaped with zero-copy Fortran order directly into the
\code{Z[pump, probe, $t_2$]} cube. Data is in m\,OD.

\paragraph{The MESS 2DIR and PyMESS 2D formats.}
A MESS 2DIR dataset is stored as a folder containing separate CSV files for each measurement component across all data states ($N_{\text{datastates}}$), spectral modulations ($N_{\text{spectra}}$), slow modulations ($N_{\text{slowmod}}$), interleaves, and population delay times ($t_2$). The reader automatically detects wobbler and chopper modulation modes, performs interferogram normalization ($I / \text{counts}$), calculates optical density $\Delta\mathrm{OD} = -1000 \log_{10}(I_{\text{probe}} / I_{\text{ref}})$, and reads the multi-file structure using parallelised, C-accelerated parsers.

For \textbf{PyMESS 2D HDF5 (\code{.h5})} datasets (\code{PyMESS\_2D} / \code{PyMESS\_2DIR}), the loader supports both time-domain (shaper and interferometer) and pre-processed frequency-domain cubes, including slow-modulation schemas where state datasets live under \code{/data/slow\_modulation/state\_XX\_<name>} (with or without \code{/data/interferograms\_raw}). Companion \code{state\_XX\_<name>\_individual\_scans} datasets are loaded into \code{Dataset2D.states\_signal\_ss} and \code{raw\_signal\_ss}. When parallel and perpendicular polarization states are present, PyMORGAN automatically derives the \code{Magic Angle} and \code{Anisotropy} state cubes — as well as their corresponding per-scan tensors — and preserves the user's active scan selection (\code{active\_scan\_indices}) across state switches via \code{set\_active\_state()}.

\subsection{Interferogram processing, phasing, and Fourier transformation}\label{sec:twoD:processing}

For raw time-domain 2D-IR measurements (such as interferometer- or pulse-shaper-based acquisitions), the data must be baseline-corrected, windowed, Fourier-transformed along the excitation axis $t_1 \to \omega_1$, and phased to produce pure absorptive 2-D correlation maps. In PyMORGAN, this is handled through \code{Dataset2D.process()} or the \emph{Phasing/FT} GUI sub-tab.

\subsubsection{Baseline correction and preprocessing}
Optical scatter and low-frequency baseline drift are removed before spectral transformation:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pulse-shaper data:} Mean subtraction across the time-domain points removes DC offsets:
    \begin{equation}
      S_{\text{corr}}(t_1, \omega_3, t_2) = S(t_1, \omega_3, t_2) - \langle S(t_1, \omega_3, t_2) \rangle_{t_1}.
    \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Interferometer data:} A running median filter with an odd kernel size of $101$ points is applied along $t_1$ to both the reference interferogram and the transient signal, isolating the oscillatory fringe signal from any slow baseline curvature.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Rotating-frame parameters}
In rotating-frame acquisitions (such as AOTF or pulse-shaper spectrometers), the experimental time step $\Delta t_1$ and rotating-frame carrier frequency $\omega_0$ (in cm$^{-1}$) define the spectral frequency axis:
\begin{equation}
  \omega_1(k) = k \cdot \Delta\nu + \omega_0, \qquad \Delta\nu = \frac{1}{N_{\text{FT}} \Delta t_1 c_0},
\end{equation}
where $c_0 = 2.99792458\times 10^{-5}$\,cm/fs is the speed of light and $N_{\text{FT}}$ is the total number of Fourier transform points. If not explicitly provided, $\omega_0$ and $\Delta t_1$ are read automatically from accompanying metadata (\code{w0.csv}). For interferometer systems, $\omega_0 = 0$ and $\Delta t_1 = 2.11079$\,fs (the HeNe laser interference fringe period).

\subsubsection{Interferogram time-zero ($t_1=0$, binzero) via the Fourier Shift Theorem}
In Fourier transform spectroscopy, determining the exact zero-path-difference position ($t_1=0$, or ``binzero'' $b_0$) is essential for obtaining pure absorptive spectra without phase distortion. Physically, placing the time origin at $t_1 = 0$ means that the phase spectrum $\phi(\omega) = \arg(\mathcal{F}\{I\})(\omega)$ has zero first derivative across the optical carrier frequency $\omega_0$:
\begin{equation}
  \left.\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\mathrm{d}\omega}\right|_{\omega_0} = 0.
\end{equation}
Rather than iteratively rolling the time-domain signal and computing hundreds of redundant Fourier transforms, PyMORGAN determines $b_0$ analytically using the \textbf{Discrete Fourier Shift Theorem}. Circularly shifting a discrete sequence of length $N$ by $b$ bins, $I'(t_1) = \operatorname{roll}(I, -b)$, corresponds to an exact linear phase ramp in the frequency domain:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}\{\operatorname{roll}(I, -b)\}[k] = X[k] \cdot \exp\left(+i \frac{2\pi k}{N} b\right),
\end{equation}
where $X[k] = \mathcal{F}\{I\}[k]$ is the base Fourier transform computed once at the start. Consequently, the unwrapped phase at frequency bin $k$ for shift $b$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_b[k] = \phi_0[k] + \left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right) b.
\end{equation}
Evaluating the phase difference across a symmetric window $[k_{\max} - \Delta k/2, k_{\max} + \Delta k/2]$ around the carrier peak gives:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\phi(b) = \phi_b[k_2] - \phi_b[k_1] = \underbrace{(\phi_0[k_2] - \phi_0[k_1])}_{\Delta\phi_0} + \underbrace{\left(\frac{2\pi \Delta k}{N}\right)}_{m} \cdot b.
\end{equation}
Because $\Delta\phi(b)$ is strictly linear in $b$, the exact binzero position $b_0$ where $\Delta\phi(b_0) \equiv 0 \pmod{2\pi}$ is solved directly in closed form:
\begin{equation}
  b_0 = \operatorname{round}\left( \frac{2\pi m_{\text{wrap}} - \Delta\phi_0}{m} \right),
\end{equation}
where $m_{\text{wrap}} = \operatorname{round}((\Delta\phi_0 + m \cdot b_{\max}) / 2\pi)$ maps to the nearest integer phase wrap at the centerburst peak $b_{\max} = \operatorname{argmax}(I)$. This closed-form solution yields exact parity with traditional multi-pass grid searches while executing in under $1\,\mu$s per delay.

\subsubsection{Apodisation (window functions)}
To eliminate truncation ripples (sinc sidelobes) arising from the finite $t_1$ acquisition window, PyMORGAN applies an apodisation window $w(q)$ centred at binzero $b_0$. For $v = |q - (b_0 + 1)| \le M = N_{\text{bins}} - b_0 - 1$, the supported window functions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Box:} $w(v) = 1.0$ (uniform Heaviside weighting).
  \item \textbf{Cosine (Cos):} $w(v) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi v}{2 M}\right)$.
  \item \textbf{Cos$^2$:} $w(v) = \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi v}{2 M}\right)$.
  \item \textbf{Cos$^3$:} $w(v) = \cos^3\left(\frac{\pi v}{2 M}\right)$.
  \item \textbf{Hanning:} $w(v) = 0.5 + 0.5 \cos\left(\frac{\pi v}{M}\right)$.
  \item \textbf{Hamming:} $w(v) = 0.54 + 0.46 \cos\left(\frac{\pi v}{M}\right)$.
\end{itemize}
Points beyond $M$ are set to zero. For one-sided acquisition modes, the symmetric window is multiplied by a Heaviside step $H(q - b_0)$ so that negative times are excluded from the single-sided Fourier transformation.

\subsubsection{Zero-padding and Fourier Transformation}
To interpolate the frequency grid and avoid spectral picket-fence distortion, zero-padding appends zeros (or the asymptotic baseline value) to extend the length from $N_{\text{bins}}$ to $N_{\text{FT}}$. PyMORGAN supports integer multiplication factors ($1\times$, $2\times$, $4\times$) and automatic padding to the next power of two ($2^k$, which accelerates the Fast Fourier Transform). The complex FFT is then computed along $t_1$ for each probe pixel and population delay.

\subsubsection{Phasing models}
The frequency-dependent phase error $\phi(\omega_1)$ is modelled by extracting the unwrapped phase spectrum around the carrier peak over a window of $\pm N$ points and fitting a polynomial by iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS):
\begin{align}
  \text{Constant:}  \quad \phi(\omega_1) &= c_0, \\
  \text{Linear:}    \quad \phi(\omega_1) &= c_1 \omega_1 + c_0, \\
  \text{Quadratic:} \quad \phi(\omega_1) &= c_2 \omega_1^2 + c_1 \omega_1 + c_0, \\
  \text{Cubic:}     \quad \phi(\omega_1) &= c_3 \omega_1^3 + c_2 \omega_1^2 + c_1 \omega_1 + c_0.
\end{align}
The phased, purely absorptive transient signal is obtained by multiplying by the complex conjugate phase factor:
\begin{equation}
  S_{\text{phased}}(\omega_1, \omega_3, t_2) = \operatorname{Re}\left( S_{\text{raw}}(\omega_1, \omega_3, t_2) \cdot e^{-i \phi(\omega_1)} \right).
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Pump correction and background subtraction}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pump correction:} When enabled, the signal at each excitation frequency $\omega_1$ is normalized by the real excitation intensity profile $|I_{\text{pump}}(\omega_1)|$, compensating for laser pulse spectral shape:
    \begin{equation}
      S_{\text{norm}}(\omega_1, \omega_3, t_2) = \frac{S_{\text{phased}}(\omega_1, \omega_3, t_2)}{|I_{\text{pump}}(\omega_1)| / \max |I_{\text{pump}}|}.
    \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Scatter subtraction:} A selected population delay map $t_2^{\text{scat}}$ (typically an early or pre-zero delay containing pump scattering without sample dynamics) can be subtracted from all subsequent maps.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Single-scan contour array exploration and scan recombination}\label{sec:twoD:singlescans}
When \code{load\_single\_scans} is enabled in the settings (\S\ref{sec:settings}), multi-scan 2-D datasets (\code{MESS\_2DIR}, \code{PyMESS\_2D}, \code{UoS\_2DIR}, and \code{RAL\_RAW}) populate \code{raw\_signal\_ss} (shape $N_{t_1/\text{pump}} \times N_{\text{probe}} \times N_{t_2} \times N_s$) and optional per-scan interferograms \code{raw\_interferogram\_ss}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Slice-level per-scan map computation (\code{compute\_scan\_maps\_at\_t2}):} Evaluates 2-D frequency-domain maps \code{(label, scan\_indices, Z\_map)} at a target $t_2$ delay for each individual scan or group of $N$ consecutive binned scans (\code{bin\_size}). For time-domain shaper or interferometer datasets, only the requested $t_2$ slice (plus the scatter-subtraction reference slice when \code{bkg\_sub=True}) is Fourier-transformed and phased per scan/bin, avoiding redundant full-cube FFTs.
  \item \textbf{Multi-panel contour array (\code{plot\_scan\_maps}):} Calls \code{pymorgan.twoD.plot.plot\_scan\_map\_array} to render a responsive grid of 2-D contour maps across all individual or binned scans alongside an \textbf{Average} comparison panel on a shared colour scale.
  \item \textbf{Scan recombination (\code{recalc\_average}):} Recomputes the dataset average across any subset of 0-based scan indices, updating \code{raw\_signal}, \code{raw\_interferogram}, \code{Z\_R}, and \code{active\_scan\_indices}.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lstlisting}
pm.update_settings(load_single_scans=True)
d2 = pm.load_2D("scan_folder", data_type="MESS_2DIR")

# Plot a grid of 2D contour maps at t2 = 0.5 ps (binning 2 scans per panel)
fig, axes, panels = d2.plot_scan_maps(0.5, bin_size=2, include_average=True)

# Recompute the dataset average excluding outlier scan index 3 (0-based)
keep = [i for i in range(d2.nscans) if i != 3]
d2.recalc_average(keep).process(apodise_method="1", phase_method="Linear")
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Background correction}
The 2-D background is a chosen $t_2$ map subtracted from the others,
\begin{equation}
  Z_C(\omega_1,\omega_3,t_2) = Z(\omega_1,\omega_3,t_2) - Z(\omega_1,\omega_3,t_2^{\text{ref}}),
\end{equation}
with the reference map itself left unchanged so it can still be inspected:

\begin{lstlisting}
data.background_correct(reference=-1)   # by t2 index
data.background_correct(t2=50)          # by nearest t2 value
data.background_correct(do_correct=False)  # passthrough
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Plotting}
\code{plot\_map} renders the map nearest a requested $t_2$ as a pump-vs-probe
contour and returns a \code{Map2DAxes(ax, top, cbar)} named tuple. It is built
\emph{from} the supplied (or freshly created) axis: the colorbar and an optional
top panel are appended with a divider, so the whole composite drops into a
parent layout.

\begin{lstlisting}
out = data.plot_map(0.5)                 # one t2 map; new figure by default
out = data.plot_map(0.5, ax=my_ax, show_colorbar=False)   # into a layout
\end{lstlisting}

Two behaviours are specific to 2-D:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Diagonal.} The $\omega_1 = \omega_3$ line is drawn only where the
        pump and probe axes overlap (pump and probe can be set independently);
        when their ranges are disjoint no diagonal is shown.
  \item \textbf{Top spectrum.} A steady-state or pump--probe spectrum can be drawn
        in a panel above the map (sharing the pump axis) via \code{top\_spectrum},
        which accepts a \code{Spectrum}, an \code{\{"X","Y"\}} mapping, or an
        \code{(x, y)} pair:
\end{itemize}

\begin{lstlisting}
ftir = pm.load_spectrum("sample_FTIR.csv", kind="absorption")
data.plot_map(0.5, top_spectrum=ftir, top_label="FTIR")
\end{lstlisting}

The same \code{show\_xlabel} / \code{show\_ylabel} / \code{show\_colorbar} /
\code{show\_colorbar\_label} toggles as in 1-D apply. The frequency-axis
labelling convention is the \code{freq\_label} setting
(\S\ref{sec:settings}): \code{Omega\_n} ($\omega_1/\omega_3$), \code{Omega\_PP}
($\omega_{\text{pump}}/\omega_{\text{probe}}$), \code{Pump-Probe}, or
\code{Omega\_n/2pic}; it can be overridden per call.

\subsubsection{Axis units and the $10^3$ rule}\label{sec:twoD:units}
The maps are stored in wavenumbers, but the axes can be displayed in any of the
units of Table~\ref{tab:2dunits}, selected with the \code{twoD\_freq\_unit}
setting or the \code{freq\_unit} argument of \code{plot\_map} and
\code{plot\_surface}. Conversions pivot through the wavelength,
\begin{equation}
  \lambda\,[\mathrm{nm}] = \frac{P_{\text{in}}}{x_{\text{in}}},
  \qquad
  x_{\text{out}} = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{\lambda\,[\mathrm{nm}]},
\end{equation}
with the constants $P$ listed in the table; wavenumbers in reciprocal inches
follow $\tilde\nu\,[\mathrm{in}^{-1}] = 2.54\,\tilde\nu\,[\mathrm{cm}^{-1}]$.

\subsection{Analysis}
The analyse stage (\code{pymorgan.twoD.analyse}) offers map and diagonal
extraction --- the pump\,=\,probe diagonal is defined over the overlap of the two
axes --- together with the spectral-diffusion metrics of
\S\ref{sec:twoD:sd} and the Kubo lineshape fitting of
\code{pymorgan.twoD.kubo\_fit}.

\begin{lstlisting}
pump, probe, m = data.slice_at(0.5)   # the 2-D map at this t2
freq, sig      = data.diagonal(0.5)   # signal along omega_1 = omega_3

cls  = data.center_line_slope()              # [t2, slope]
both = data.center_line_slope(both=True)     # [t2, CLS, IvCLS]
nls  = data.nodal_line_slope()               # [t2, slope]
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Spectral diffusion}\label{sec:twoD:sd}
The centre-line slope (CLS) is obtained by taking, for every pump frequency
$\omega_1$, the extremum of the slice along the probe axis and fitting a line
through those points; the inverse CLS (IvCLS) swaps the roles of the two axes,
and the nodal-line slope (NLS) tracks the zero crossing between the bleach and
the excited-state absorption. The line is fitted robustly (Huber loss), so a few
outlying slices do not tilt the result.

\subsubsection{Sub-pixel refinement}
The extremum of a slice is \emph{not} taken at the sampled maximum: with probe
pixels of a few cm$^{-1}$ that would quantise the centre line and bias the
slope. Instead the position is obtained in two stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the slice is interpolated onto a grid \code{sd\_interpolation\_factor}
        times finer with a cubic spline, and the extremum is located there,
        already resolving positions below the spectral resolution;
  \item a local model --- \code{sd\_interpolation}: a quadratic, cubic or
        quartic polynomial, or a Gaussian or Lorentzian peak --- is fitted by
        least squares to the \emph{measured} points around that location, and
        its stationary point is the reported centre.
\end{enumerate}
Fitting the measured samples (rather than applying a three-point formula to the
interpolated curve) keeps the estimate continuous and averages the noise over
several points. On a synthetic tilted trough sampled at 2.5\,cm$^{-1}$ the
residual error is $\approx0.01$ pixel for the quadratic model and
$\approx0.003$ pixel for the quartic one; with \code{sd\_interpolation = "none"}
the fine-grid position is returned unrefined, and its resolution is set by the
interpolation factor alone.

Two further settings restrict what enters the fit:
\code{sd\_peak\_range} (cm$^{-1}$) is the half-width of the search window around
the coarse extremum, \code{sd\_intensity\_threshold} the minimum amplitude
(relative to the strongest signal of the map) a slice must reach to contribute,
and \code{sd\_peak\_type} selects the bleach minimum or the excited-state
absorption maximum.

The decay of the extracted slopes against $t_2$ is plotted from the GUI with
single- or bi-exponential fits. That figure always uses a \emph{linear} $t_2$
axis: population times are a short, linearly sampled series, unlike the wide
delay range of a 1-D experiment.

\subsection{Graphical interface}\label{sec:twoD:gui}

The GUI (\code{pymorgan-gui}) exposes the full 2-D pipeline through the
\textbf{2D} tab.  The left column contains a hierarchical file browser,
format selector, and a series of sub-tabs that drive processing and

\subsubsection{Other plt. sub-tab}

The \emph{Other plt.} sub-tab contains three diagnostic views selected
by the \textsc{td} / \textsc{ph} / \textsc{cal} toggle buttons:

\begin{description}
  \item[TD] Time-domain interferogram and signal for the selected
    probe pixel.
  \item[PH] Fourier transform amplitude (black), a Gaussian fit
    (green dashed), and — for interferometer data — the unwrapped
    phase (red dots) and the polynomial fit (blue line) on a twin
    $y$-axis.  The $x$-axis is locked to $\pm 2\times\mathrm{FWHM}$
    of the Gaussian fit.  The phase axis is re-centred so the fitted
    phase is zero at the spectral peak, wrapped to $[-\pi,\pi]$, and
    plotted with fixed limits $[-\pi,\pi]$.
  \item[CAL] Calibrated probe axis (pixel vs.\ wavenumber / THz).
\end{description}

Clicking the \textbf{Pop} button opens an independent, resizable window
that mirrors the same axes.  The window is linked to the main GUI: it
updates whenever the delay or plot mode changes.  Closing the pop-out
does not affect the embedded plot.

\subsubsection{Direct Kubo Lineshape \& Generalized FFCF Fitting}\label{sec:twoD:kubo}

The \textbf{Kubo Fit\ldots} dialog (\code{kubo\_dialog.py}, backed by \code{pymorgan.twoD.kubo\_fit}) performs full third-order response-function simulation and multi-core non-linear least-squares fitting of 2D spectra using generalized Frequency Fluctuation Correlation Functions (FFCFs):
\begin{equation}
  C(t) = \Delta_0^2 + \sum_{m=1}^{M} \Delta_m^2 e^{-t/\tau_{c,m}}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding line-broadening function $g(t)$ combines pure homogeneous dephasing ($\Delta\omega_{T_2}$, with $1/T_2^* = \pi c_0 \Delta\omega_{T_2}$), static inhomogeneous broadening ($\Delta_0$), and an arbitrary number of Kubo exponential decay terms ($\Delta_m$, correlation time $\tau_{c,m}$):
\begin{equation}
  g(t) = \pi c_0 \Delta\omega_{T_2}\, t + \frac{1}{2}\sigma_0^2 t^2 + \sum_{m=1}^{M} \sigma_m^2 \tau_{c,m}^2 \left( e^{-t/\tau_{c,m}} + \frac{t}{\tau_{c,m}} - 1 \right),
  \qquad \sigma_i = \frac{2\pi c_0 \, \Delta_i}{2\sqrt{2\ln 2}}.
\end{equation}
Key capabilities of the engine and dialog include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Shared or per-band independent FFCFs:} Multiple FFCF groups can be defined and assigned independently to the excitation ($\omega_1$, \code{ffcf\_id}) and detection ($\omega_3$, \code{ffcf\_id\_w3}) coordinates of each oscillator band.
  \item \textbf{Diagonal and cross-peak bands with bath correlation:} Supports both diagonal oscillators ($\omega_1 = \omega_3$) and off-diagonal cross-peaks ($\omega_1 \ne \omega_3$) with parameter linking (\code{link\_w1}, \code{link\_w3}, \code{link\_anharm}) and a tunable cross-peak bath correlation factor $\eta_{AB} \in [-1, 1]$.
  \item \textbf{Variable Projection (VarPro) \& multi-core parallelisation:} Uses Trust-Region Reflective (\code{scipy.optimize.least\_squares}) with analytical Variable Projection to solve the linear GSB and ESA pathway amplitudes ($A_{\text{GSB}}, A_{\text{ESA}}$) at each evaluation, alongside full-parameter TRF and \code{L-BFGS-B} solvers. Time-domain evaluation uses separable 1D outer products, batched 2D FFTs, separable 2D rectilinear interpolation, and multi-core parallel execution across $t_2$ delays (\code{ProcessPool} / \code{ThreadPool}) with parallel Jacobian column evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Joint 2D + 1st-order FTIR fitting \& diagnostics:} Simultaneously fits 2D correlation maps across selected $t_2$ delays and the analytical 1st-order steady-state FTIR spectrum (weighted by \code{kubo\_ftir\_weight}), with live FFCF $C(t)$ and $g(t)$ diagnostic plots and JSON/TOML model preset import/export.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Interactive 2D Gaussian Peak Fitting \& Cross-Peak Generator}\label{sec:twoD:gaussian}

The \textbf{2D Gaussian Fit\ldots} dialog (\code{twoD\_gaussian\_dialog.py}, backed by \code{fit\_2d\_gaussian\_map} and \code{fit\_2d\_gaussian\_global}) fits a sum of 2D correlated elliptical Gaussian peaks to diagonal and off-diagonal features:
\begin{equation}
  G(\omega_1, \omega_3) = A \exp\!\left( -\frac{1}{2(1-\rho^2)} \left[ \frac{(\omega_1 - \omega_{1,0})^2}{\sigma_1^2} + \frac{(\omega_3 - \omega_{3,0})^2}{\sigma_3^2} - \frac{2\rho(\omega_1 - \omega_{1,0})(\omega_3 - \omega_{3,0})}{\sigma_1 \sigma_3} \right] \right).
\end{equation}
Users can click directly on the 2D contour map to seed peaks, automatically generate linked cross-peak pairs from diagonal modes via the \code{CrossPeakGeneratorDialog}, link centers/anharmonicities across peaks, and fit either single $t_2$ slices or globally across the entire $t_2$ stack.

\subsubsection{2D ROI Volume Integration \& Dynamics}\label{sec:twoD:integral}

The \textbf{2D Integral Dynamics\ldots} dialog (\code{twoD\_integral\_dialog.py}) allows interactive rectangular region-of-interest (ROI) selection on the $(\omega_1, \omega_3)$ map using a draggable \code{RectangleSelector}. It integrates the 2D signal volume $I(t_2) = \iint_{\mathrm{ROI}} Z(\omega_1, \omega_3, t_2)\,\mathrm{d}\omega_1\,\mathrm{d}\omega_3$ across all population waiting times $t_2$, plots the resulting kinetic trace, and fits it with single- or multi-exponential decay models.

\subsubsection{2D Spectrum Subtraction}\label{sec:twoD:subtraction}

The \textbf{Subtract 2D Spectra\ldots} dialog (\code{twoD\_subtraction\_dialog.py}) loads a reference 2D dataset (such as pure solvent or buffer), interpolates it onto the sample dataset's $(\omega_1, \omega_3, t_2)$ grid, and performs scaled subtraction $Z_{\mathrm{corr}} = Z_{\mathrm{sample}} - k \, Z_{\mathrm{ref}}$ with a live three-panel preview (Sample, Scaled Reference, and Difference Map).
