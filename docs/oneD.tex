\section{One-dimensional data}\label{sec:oneD}

\subsection{Pipeline overview}
The one-dimensional branch is organised as a \emph{load $\to$ process $\to$
plot $\to$ analyse} pipeline. Each stage is a module under
\code{pymorgan.oneD}, and the \code{Dataset1D} object ties them together:
it holds the raw arrays and exposes one set of methods per stage. The stage
functions take the dataset as their first argument, so they remain reusable on
their own and new routines integrate with the whole pipeline. Table~\ref{tab:stages}
maps each stage to its module and entry points.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    Stage & Module & Entry point(s) \\
    \midrule
    load    & \code{pymorgan.oneD.load}    & \code{pm.load\_1D}, \code{Dataset1D.from\_file} \\
    process & \code{pymorgan.oneD.process} / \code{scatter} & \code{Dataset1D.background\_correct}, \code{remove\_scatter} \\
            & \code{pymorgan.oneD.chirp} / \code{stitch} / \code{svd} & \code{apply\_chirp\_correction}, \code{compute\_svd} \\
    plot    & \code{pymorgan.oneD.plot}    & \code{Dataset1D.plot\_*} \\
    analyse & \textit{(PyRATE-TA)} & \textit{separate project} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{The 1-D pipeline stages and where they live.}
  \label{tab:stages}
\end{table}

\subsection{Data model}
A one-dimensional measurement is represented by a \code{Dataset1D} object,
which bundles the arrays produced by a loader. The signal is stored as a
three-dimensional array to generalise over multiple detectors,
\begin{equation}
  Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\tau \times N_\lambda \times N_d},
\end{equation}
indexed by delay $\tau$, probe coordinate $\lambda$ (wavelength, wavenumber or
energy) and detector $d$. The accompanying axes are the delay vector
$\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\tau}$ and the probe vector
$\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\lambda}$. Table~\ref{tab:fields} lists the seven
fields every loader returns.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    Field & Symbol / shape & Meaning \\
    \midrule
    \code{Zavg\_R} & $N_\tau \times N_\lambda \times N_d$ & averaged signal \\
    \code{delays}  & $N_\tau$ & delay axis \\
    \code{probe}   & $N_\lambda$ & probe (spectral) axis \\
    \code{Units}   & dict & unit labels (see \S\ref{sec:settings}) \\
    \code{Nscans}  & scalar & number of scans (\code{nan} if unknown) \\
    \code{Zss\_R}  & $N_\tau \times N_\lambda \times N_d \times N_s$ & single-scan signal \\
    \code{Zstdv}   & $N_\tau \times N_\lambda$ & standard deviation \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Fields returned by a 1-D loader and stored on \code{Dataset1D}.}
  \label{tab:fields}
\end{table}

The most-processed signal is exposed through the \code{Z} property, which
returns the background-corrected array once a correction has been applied and
falls back to the raw array otherwise.

\subsection{Loading and the loader registry}
Data formats are decoupled from the dataset through a \emph{loader registry}
(\code{pymorgan.oneD.load}). A loader is any callable that maps a file path to
the seven fields of Table~\ref{tab:fields}. Loaders are registered under one or
more data-type names and resolved at load time:

\begin{lstlisting}
import pymorgan as pm

data = pm.load_1D("scan.pdat", data_type="PDAT")
print(pm.available_loaders())
\end{lstlisting}

A new format is added by decorating a reader with \code{@pm.register\_loader("MyInstrument")}:

\begin{lstlisting}

def read_my_instrument(path):
    ...
    return Zavg_R, delays, probe, Units, Nscans, Zss_R, Zstdv
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{High-performance file parsing.}
Large experimental files (such as UniGE fsTA and Helios TA scans spanning tens of thousands of lines) are parsed using high-throughput C-accelerated engines (\code{pandas.read\_csv} with \code{engine="c"}) with automated fallbacks for non-standard whitespace or formatting anomalies. Probe wavelength calibration is automatically resolved from local directory structures (e.g.\ \code{CalibratedProbe.csv} or \code{pix2lam.mat}).

\paragraph{The PDAT format.}
A \code{.pdat} file is comma-separated. The first line carries the time and
probe units separated by an asterisk (e.g.\ \code{ps*cm\textasciicircum\{-1\}}).
The first data row holds the probe axis (with a leading $0$), and each
subsequent row begins with a delay value followed by the signal at every probe
coordinate. PDAT files store averaged data already in m\,OD, so no rescaling is
applied on read. Per-point standard deviations are optionally stored in a sibling
\code{.pdatn} file with an identical layout.

\paragraph{PyMESS HDF5 slow-modulation and multi-signal layouts.}
The PyMESS HDF5 readers (\code{PyMESS\_PP}, \code{PyMESS\_Aniso}, and \code{PyMESS\_t2scan}) automatically classify and parse both standard single-state pump--probe datasets and multi-state acquisition schemas:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Slow modulation (\code{/data/slow\_modulation/state\_XX\_<name>}):} State datasets are discovered while filtering out their \code{*\_individual\_scans} companions from state enumeration. Per-state single scans (\code{state\_XX\_<name>\_individual\_scans}) are loaded into \code{Zss\_R} (shape $N_\tau \times N_\lambda \times N_{\text{states}} \times N_{\text{scans}}$) and used to compute a true empirical per-point standard deviation \code{Zstdv}. For polarization anisotropy datasets (\code{PyMESS\_Aniso}), the isotropic Magic Angle ($\Delta A_{\mathrm{iso}} = (\Delta A_\parallel + 2G\,\Delta A_\perp)/3$) and Anisotropy ($r = (\Delta A_\parallel - G\,\Delta A_\perp)/(\Delta A_\parallel + 2G\,\Delta A_\perp)$) channels are appended automatically alongside the parallel and perpendicular states, including their derived per-scan tensors.
  \item \textbf{Multi-signal fast modulation (\code{/data/signals/signal\_XX\_*}):} Multi-beam or multi-phase chop/modulation schemes stored under \code{/data/signals} (such as pump-only, push-only, and pump+push difference channels) are exposed as named detector/modulation states in \code{Units["detectors"]}, with companion \code{*\_noise} datasets mapped directly to \code{Zstdv}.
  \item \textbf{Shaper population-time scans (\code{PyMESS\_t2scan}):} 2D-IR waiting-time ($t_2$) scans acquired at a fixed or zero coherence time ($t_1 = 0$) are supported whether raw interferograms are stored under \code{/data/interferograms\_raw} or pre-demodulated state matrices are stored under \code{/data/slow\_modulation}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Background correction}
The pre-time-zero background is estimated by averaging the signal over a delay
window $[\tau_\mathrm{min},\tau_\mathrm{max}]$ and subtracting it from every
delay,
\begin{equation}
  Z_C(\tau,\lambda,d) = Z(\tau,\lambda,d)
    - \big\langle Z(\tau',\lambda,d) \big\rangle_{\tau' \in [\tau_\mathrm{min},\tau_\mathrm{max}]},
\end{equation}
where the average is taken with \code{nan}-aware statistics
(\code{pymorgan.oneD.process}). The operation is applied through

\begin{lstlisting}
data.background_correct(tmin=-20, tmax=-5)          # apply
data.background_correct(tmin=-20, tmax=-5, do_correct=False)  # passthrough
\end{lstlisting}

With \code{do\_correct=False} the raw signal is copied unchanged into the
corrected fields, so downstream code can always read \code{Z} uniformly. The
method returns \code{self}, allowing calls to be chained. For datasets plagued
by time-varying, drifting, or fluctuating pump scatter that static pre-zero
subtraction fails to cancel, use the dynamic multi-band algorithm described
next (\S\ref{sec:scatter}).

\subsection{Advanced scatter and dynamic background removal}\label{sec:scatter}
Standard static pre-zero subtraction removes a single time-independent
spectrum averaged over $[\tau_{\mathrm{min}}, \tau_{\mathrm{max}}]$. In
real ultrafast transient absorption experiments, pump scatter and broadband
pre-zero backgrounds violate that static assumption for four physical reasons:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Delay-stage scatter dimming:} Movement of the mechanical
        delay stage slightly steers or clips the pump/probe overlap and changes
        the scattered pump intensity over nanoseconds. Subtracting the fixed
        negative-delay peak oversubtracts at positive delays, creating a
        phantom positive band across the pump core.
  \item \textbf{Delay-dependent spectral walk:} The centroid or width of the
        scatter feature shifts subtly with delay stage position or spectrometer
        slit illumination, turning residuals into derivative-like dispersive
        bipolar artefacts.
  \item \textbf{Shot-to-shot and scan-to-scan jitter:} Flicker between delays
        and laser/sample scatter drift between individual scans leave noisy
        stripes in the averaged map and severely inflate the per-point inter-scan
        standard deviation \code{Zstdv} around the scatter peak, distorting
        weighted global fits in \textbf{PyRATE-TA}.
  \item \textbf{Broadband fluorescence and far-wing dimming:} Slow delay
        evolution of fluorescence or static scatter wings creates an apparent
        broadband drift across all wavelengths if subtracted statically.
\end{enumerate}

To eliminate all four artefacts without suppressing genuine excited-state
absorption, bleach, or coherent time-zero signals underneath the pump band,
PyMORGAN provides \code{pymorgan.oneD.scatter} (exposed via
\code{compute\_scatter\_removal}, \code{detect\_scatter\_bands}, and the
\code{Dataset1D} methods \code{detect\_scatter\_bands}, \code{remove\_scatter},
\code{apply\_scatter\_removal}, and \code{clear\_scatter\_removal}).

\paragraph{Two-tier automatic band detection (\code{detect\_scatter\_bands}).}
From the pre-zero averaged spectrum $B_{\mathrm{pre}}(\lambda)$ over
$[\tau_{\mathrm{min}}, \tau_{\mathrm{max}}]$ (defaulting to $\tau \le -1$, or
the earliest negative delays), localised bands are identified in two passes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pass 1 (primary bands):} A median filter of odd window size
        $k = \max(21, \lfloor 0.08\,N_{\lambda} \rceil_{\mathrm{odd}})$ separates
        the smooth broadband background from sharp scatter features. A robust
        noise estimate $\sigma = 1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD}$ sets a threshold
        $\max(6\sigma, \text{floor})$ (where the floor is $0.5\,\mathrm{mOD}$ or
        $5 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{OD}$). Contiguous runs of at least $3$ pixels
        (bridging 1--2 pixel gaps) are expanded outward pixel by pixel until the
        signal drops below $\max(2\sigma, 1\%\,\text{peak})$, merged if
        overlapping, and sorted by peak amplitude so Band~0 is the dominant
        primary scatter band. Negative scatter dips are detected by default;
        setting \code{allow\_positive=True} detects signed artefacts of either
        polarity.
  \item \textbf{Pass 2 (2nd-order grating harmonics):} When the probe axis is
        calibrated in wavelength ($\mathrm{nm}$), PyMORGAN inspects a
        $\pm 12\,\mathrm{nm}$ search window around $2\lambda_{\mathrm{pump}}$
        (obtained from \code{pymess\_metadata["pump\_wl\_nm"]} or the Band~0
        peak) for second-order diffraction dips. Candidates with peak
        $\mathrm{SNR} \ge 5\sigma$ are added as enabled rank-1 coupled bands
        (\code{couple\_to=0}), whereas $3\sigma\text{--}5\sigma$ candidates are
        appended disabled (\code{enabled=False}) as user suggestions.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mathematical decomposition (\code{compute\_scatter\_removal}).}
Each scatter band $m$ defines a core interval $[\lambda_{\min,m}, \lambda_{\max,m}]$,
a wing margin $w_{\mathrm{wing},m}$ (default $\max(3\Delta\lambda, 0.5\,\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{band}})$),
and a raised-cosine taper width $w_{\mathrm{taper},m}$ (default
$\min(w_{\mathrm{wing},m}, \max(2\Delta\lambda, 0.25\,\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{band}}))$):
\begin{equation}
  T_m(\lambda) =
  \begin{cases}
    1, & \lambda \in [\lambda_{\min,m}, \lambda_{\max,m}], \\
    \frac{1}{2}\left[1 - \cos\!\left(\pi\frac{\lambda - (\lambda_{\min,m} - w_{\mathrm{taper},m})}{w_{\mathrm{taper},m}}\right)\right], & \lambda \in [\lambda_{\min,m} - w_{\mathrm{taper},m}, \lambda_{\min,m}), \\
    \frac{1}{2}\left[1 - \cos\!\left(\pi\frac{(\lambda_{\max,m} + w_{\mathrm{taper},m}) - \lambda}{w_{\mathrm{taper},m}}\right)\right], & \lambda \in (\lambda_{\max,m}, \lambda_{\max,m} + w_{\mathrm{taper},m}], \\
    0, & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Uncoupled bands whose extended fitting windows
$[\lambda_{\min,m} - w_{\mathrm{wing},m}, \lambda_{\max,m} + w_{\mathrm{wing},m}]$
touch or overlap are automatically merged into joint groups. Within each group's
fitting window, a linear baseline $b_{\mathrm{wing}}(\lambda)$ estimated from the
core-free wings isolates the strictly localised pre-zero template
$P_0(\lambda) = (B_{\mathrm{pre}}(\lambda) - b_{\mathrm{wing}}(\lambda))\,T(\lambda)$,
yielding the complementary broadband background
\begin{equation}
  B_{\mathrm{broad}}(\lambda) = B_{\mathrm{pre}}(\lambda) - \sum_{m} P_{0,m}(\lambda).
\end{equation}
When \code{scale\_broadband=True} (default), a slow Savitzky--Golay-smoothed
amplitude envelope $a_{\mathrm{smooth}}(\tau)$ of the strongest primary band
(re-normalised so its pre-zero median equals $1.0$ and clipped to $[0.1, 2.5]$)
scales $B_{\mathrm{broad}}$ across delays prior to local scatter removal,
\begin{equation}
  Y_{\mathrm{nb}}(\tau, \lambda) = Y(\tau, \lambda) - a_{\mathrm{smooth}}(\tau)\, B_{\mathrm{broad}}(\lambda),
\end{equation}
removing far-wing and fluorescence dimming without injecting fast laser jitter.

Inside each uncoupled group's fitting window, PyMORGAN then solves at every delay
$\tau$ a joint linear least-squares system separating a tapered scatter basis
$\{\phi_r(\lambda)\}_{r=1}^{R}$ from a low-degree local polynomial representing
the smooth physical TA spectrum beneath the band:
\begin{equation}
  Y_{\mathrm{nb}}(\tau, \lambda) \approx \sum_{r=1}^{R} c_r(\tau)\,\phi_r(\lambda) + \sum_{j=0}^{p} \beta_j(\tau)\,\tilde{\lambda}^j,
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\lambda} \in [-1, 1]$ is the normalised local probe coordinate and
$p = \code{poly\_deg}$ (default $2$). Only the scatter term
$\sum_r c_r(\tau)\,\phi_r(\lambda)$ is subtracted inside the tapered window;
the smooth physical TA component $\sum_j \beta_j(\tau)\,\tilde{\lambda}^j$ is
preserved intact. Three basis modes are supported via \code{method}:
\begin{description}
  \item[\code{local\_svd} (Adaptive Local SVD, default)] Estimates the smooth
        TA trend from the side wings alone, subtracts it, applies the taper
        $T(\lambda)$, and computes a truncated SVD over delays outside
        \code{t0\_exclude} (default $(-0.5, 1.5)$) so coherent artefacts near
        $t_0$ never contaminate the scatter modes. The leading $R$ (\code{rank},
        default $3$) right singular vectors are re-tapered to form
        $\{\phi_r(\lambda)\}$, oriented to match $P_0(\lambda)$, and solved
        jointly per delay.
  \item[\code{taylor} (Pre-Zero Template + Derivatives)] Constructs a strictly
        pre-zero basis from $P_0(\lambda)$ and its tapered cubic-spline
        spectral derivatives $[P_0,\, P_0'T,\, P_0''T,\, \dots]$ up to order
        $R-1$, directly absorbing amplitude dimming and spectral walk.
  \item[\code{warped} (Warped Pre-Zero Template)] Performs strictly pre-zero
        nonlinear Variable Projection (VARPRO) per delay using
        \code{scipy.optimize.least\_squares}, optimising spectral shift
        $\delta\lambda(\tau)$ and optional width stretch $\gamma(\tau)$ (when
        $R \ge 2$) on a cubic-spline interpolant
        $P_0(\lambda_{\mathrm{c}} + (\lambda - \lambda_{\mathrm{c}} - \delta\lambda)/\gamma)\,T(\lambda)$
        while eliminating linear amplitudes and the local TA polynomial in
        closed form.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Harmonic coupling, per-scan noise update, and optical options.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Harmonic coupling (\code{couple\_to}):} A weak harmonic band
        with \code{couple\_to=target\_idx} avoids overfitting noisy single
        delays by modelling its scatter purely as $\alpha\,a_{\mathrm{smooth}}^{\mathrm{target}}(\tau)\,P_{0,c}(\lambda)$,
        where a single global scalar $\alpha \in [0, 2.5]$ is fitted outside
        \code{t0\_exclude}.
  \item \textbf{Per-scan cleaning and \code{Zstdv} update:} When single-scan
        data (\code{Zss\_R}) exist and \code{per\_scan=True} (default), every
        scan is cleaned independently before recomputing the averaged signal
        \code{Zavg\_C}. With \code{update\_noise=True} (default), \code{Zstdv}
        is recomputed across the cleaned scans (\code{ddof=1}), removing
        scatter-drift inflation for downstream reduced-$\chi^2$ fitting in
        \textbf{PyRATE-TA}. Calling \code{clear\_scatter\_removal()} restores
        the original \code{Zstdv}.
  \item \textbf{Transmittance space and saturation masking:} Setting
        \code{transmittance\_space=True} transforms raw $\Delta A$ into linear
        transmittance modulation $Y = 10^{-\Delta A/s} - 1$ ($s = 1000$ for
        $\mathrm{mOD}$, $1$ for $\mathrm{OD}$) before decomposition and maps
        back via $-s\log_{10}(1 + Y_{\mathrm{out}})$. When detector saturation
        leaves unrecoverable pixels, \code{mask\_saturated=True} sets cells
        inside enabled cores with $|Z_{\mathrm{raw}}| > \code{saturation\_threshold}$
        (default $500\,\mathrm{mOD}$) to \code{NaN}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Recommended order of operations.}
Run advanced scatter removal \textbf{before} chirp correction
(\S\ref{sec:chirp}). Pump scatter is physically tied to the optical delay-stage
position and is unchirped, whereas chirp interpolation smears sharp scatter
features across multiple pixels and mixes delays.

\begin{lstlisting}
# 1. Inspect auto-detected bands (native probe units, e.g. nm)
bands = data.detect_scatter_bands(tmin=-20, tmax=-2)

# 2. Apply dynamic multi-band removal in place (per-scan + Zstdv update)
data.remove_scatter(method="local_svd", rank=3, per_scan=True, update_noise=True)

# Or run explicitly to inspect diagnostics and before/after quality metrics
from pymorgan.oneD.scatter import ScatterBand, compute_scatter_removal
res = compute_scatter_removal(
    data,
    bands=[ScatterBand(348.2, 362.4, label="Primary"),
           ScatterBand(704.5, 715.3, rank=1, couple_to=0, label="2nd order")],
    method="local_svd",
)
print(res.metrics)
data.apply_scatter_removal(res)
# data.clear_scatter_removal()  # restores original Zstdv if reverting
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Solvent subtraction}\label{sec:solvent}
For measurements in solution, the pure-solvent response often has to be
subtracted. The module exposes two entry points.

\paragraph{Manual subtraction.} A scaled, time-shifted solvent trace is removed
from the sample signal:
\begin{lstlisting}
data.subtract_solvent(solvent, dt=0.0, scale=1.0)
\end{lstlisting}
\code{dt} shifts the solvent delay axis before interpolation (accounts for a
timing offset between the two measurements) and \code{scale} multiplies the
solvent signal before subtraction.

\paragraph{Automatic solvent fitting.} \code{fit\_solvent\_auto} models the
solvent contribution pixel by pixel using the solvent trace convolved with the
Instrument Response Function (IRF) and removes it:
\begin{lstlisting}
fit = data.fit_solvent_auto(solvent)
data.subtract_solvent_fit(fit)
\end{lstlisting}
Optional per-pixel fits for the time-zero offset and the amplitude scale can be
enabled through the \code{per\_pixel\_dt} and \code{per\_pixel\_scale} settings
(Section~\ref{sec:settings}); the defaults are \code{false} and \code{true},
respectively.

\subsection{Shockwave subtraction}\label{sec:shockwave}
In transient IR / UV-Vis measurements using flowing samples, acoustic shockwaves
generated by the pump beam can appear as a time-dependent artefact that is
correlated across \emph{all} probe wavelengths. The artefact is removed by
averaging a selected subset of detector pixels (or a probe-axis range) into a
one-dimensional shockwave trace and subtracting it from every pixel:
\begin{lstlisting}
from pymorgan.oneD.process import subtract_shockwave

Z_corrected = subtract_shockwave(Z, pixels=[0, 1, 2])   # pixel indices (0-based)
\end{lstlisting}

The GUI exposes this through the \textbf{Subtract Shockwave\ldots} button in the
1-D tab, which opens the \code{ShockwaveSubtractionDialog}. The dialog lets the
user choose between pixel-index and probe-axis-value selection, displays a live
preview of the proposed shockwave trace, and applies the correction on
confirmation (see \S\ref{sec:oneD:gui:dialogs}).

\subsection{Chirp (dispersion) correction}\label{sec:chirp}
Ultrafast time-resolved spectroscopy measurements often suffer from Group Delay Dispersion (GDD), commonly referred to as ``chirp''. As the probe pulse passes through various dispersive optical elements (such as white-light generation crystals, lenses, sample cell windows, and the solvent itself), different spectral components are temporally stretched. Consequently, the effective time-zero $t_0(\lambda)$ varies across the probe wavelength range $\lambda$.

PyMORGAN provides tools to characterise and correct this dispersion. The time-zero profile $t_0(\lambda)$ is modelled using a generalised Cauchy dispersion equation:
\begin{equation}
  t_0(\lambda) = c_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{N_C-1} c_n \left(\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\lambda}\right)^{2n},
\end{equation}
where $c_0, \dots, c_{N_C-1}$ are the dispersion coefficients, $\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a reference wavelength (which defaults to the mean of the fitted range to make the coefficients dimensionless and of comparable magnitude), and $N_C$ is the number of terms (usually $6$).

To determine $t_0(\lambda)$ across the probe range, PyMORGAN implements three fit modes in the \code{pymorgan.oneD.chirp} module:
\begin{description}
  \item[Automatic] Fits the coherent artefact and early dynamics at every pixel independently. The model consists of a Gaussian Instrument Response Function (IRF), its optional first and second derivatives, a constant offset, and an optional erf-broadened exponential decay. PyMORGAN uses Variable Projection (VARPRO) to eliminate the linear amplitudes in closed form, optimizing only the nonlinear parameters $(t_0, \mathrm{FWHM}, \tau_{\mathrm{exp}})$ per pixel. A second pass uses smoothed parameters from the first pass as initial guesses to improve stability.
  \item[Step function] A simplified alternative that jointly fits a Gaussian IRF (+ derivatives) and a Heaviside step convolved with the same Gaussian over a narrow early-time window. This is robust when the early-time exponential decay is not needed or is ill-conditioned.
  \item[Manual] Fits the Cauchy dispersion equation directly to a set of user-picked $(\lambda, t_0)$ points (e.g., clicked interactively on the contour map in the GUI), bypassing the per-pixel IRF fitting.
\end{description}

Once $t_0(\lambda)$ is obtained, the Cauchy dispersion equation is fit using bound-constrained, iteratively reweighted linear least-squares (IRLS with Tukey's bisquare weights) to handle outliers and reject bad pixel fits.

To apply the correction, the dataset's delay axes are interpolated onto a common chirp-free delay grid. Two boundary handling strategies are available:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{crop}: Slices the delay axis to keep only the delay region where all wavelength channels are defined, discarding the out-of-bounds regions that become \code{NaN} after shifting.
  \item \code{extrapolate}: Fills out-of-bound values with the nearest valid boundary endpoint value, keeping the original delay range.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lstlisting}
# Fit chirp automatically and apply the correction
fit = data.fit_chirp_automatic(deriv_mask=(True, True), use_exp=True)
data.plot_chirp_diagnostics(fit)
data.apply_chirp_correction(fit, boundary_handling="crop")
\end{lstlisting}

data.plot_spectra([0.5, 1, 5, 20, 100], doSmooth=1)
ax, t, Y = data.plot_kinetics([2132, 2218], plotStyle="-")

from pymorgan.oneD import plot                          # stage-function form
plot.plot_contour(data, label_style="/", cmap_ID="Rd/Wh/Bu v2")
\end{lstlisting}

The label style, colourmap, time-axis scale and \dA{} unit convention are taken
from the active settings (\S\ref{sec:settings}); any of them can be overridden
per figure through the corresponding keyword argument.

For datasets with high dynamic range where weak features would otherwise be washed out by dominant bleach or ESA peaks, \code{plot\_contour} provides non-linear $\operatorname{arcsinh}$ colour scaling via \code{Asinh=True} (also toggled in the GUI plot controls). Rather than modifying the underlying signal values, PyMORGAN applies an inverse hyperbolic sine colour normalization (\code{matplotlib.colors.AsinhNorm}) paired with non-linear $\sinh$-spaced contour levels:
\begin{equation}
  z_k = s \cdot \sinh(u_k), \quad u_k \in \left[-\operatorname{arcsinh}\left(\frac{|Z_{\min}|}{s}\right), \operatorname{arcsinh}\left(\frac{Z_{\max}}{s}\right)\right],
\end{equation}
where $s = \frac{p}{100} \cdot \max(|Z_{\min}|, |Z_{\max}|)$ is the linear crossover threshold set by \code{asinh\_pct} ($p$, default $5.0\%$). Signals within $\pm s$ behave linearly, while larger amplitudes are compressed logarithmically. Because the underlying data array is not mutated, coordinates and hover values retain their physical units ($\mathrm{mOD}$), and colorbar ticks remain linear and clean (centred at zero and rounded to sensible numbers).

By default every plotter creates its own figure and axis. Passing an existing
axis through \code{ax=} draws into it instead, which is how panels are combined
into a layout or several datasets are overlaid on one axis. Repeated elements
(axis labels and, for the maps, the colorbar) can be switched off so they are
not drawn more than once; the toggles are listed in Table~\ref{tab:toggles}.

  \label{tab:toggles}
\end{table}

# two maps side by side; suppress the duplicated colorbar / y-axis label
dataA.plot_contour(ax=axL, show_colorbar=False)

\code{pm.show\_plots(block=False)} returns immediately (e.g.\ inside a GUI event

\subsection{Kinetic analysis}\label{sec:analyse}
Kinetic analysis is \emph{not} part of PyMORGAN. Trace extraction and fitting
--- multi-exponential decays, and full global/target analysis with rate-matrix
models (the source of the EAS/SAS consumed by \code{plot\_species\_spectra})
--- live in the companion project \textbf{PyRATE-TA}, which shares this
loader/dataset layer.

PyMORGAN keeps the two ends of that seam. Traces are obtained from
\code{plot\_kinetics}, whose third return value is the extracted data itself:

\begin{lstlisting}
ax, t, Y = data.plot_kinetics([1950, 2000], plotStyle="-")
Y.shape                                           # [Ndelays x Ncuts]
\end{lstlisting}

and fitted spectra are rendered by \code{plot\_species\_spectra}, which takes
plain arrays (\code{Sfit}, \code{Taus}, \code{TauErr}, \code{isFixTau},
\code{modelType}) and is therefore independent of how the fit was produced.

The third piece of the seam is the noise. \code{Dataset1D.noise\_array()}
returns the per-point standard deviation --- from \code{Zstdv} when a sibling
\code{.pdatn} file was loaded, otherwise the spread across single scans, and
\code{None} when the dataset carries neither:

\begin{lstlisting}
sigma = data.noise_array()      # [Ndelays x Npixels x Ndetectors], or None
\end{lstlisting}

A weighted fit uses it as \code{1/sigma}, which is what turns a fit statistic
into a true reduced $\chi^2$; without it the statistic is a plain sum of
squared residuals. PyMORGAN owns the noise because it owns the loaders; PyRATE-TA
never estimates it.

\subsection{Settings and aesthetics}\label{sec:settings}
Presentation choices that are not intrinsic to the data are collected in a
single \code{Settings} object. It is framework-agnostic: the GUI binds to the
fields it describes, but the package never depends on the GUI. The fields are
summarised in Table~\ref{tab:settings}.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    Field & Values & Effect \\
    \midrule
    \code{profile} & regular / poster / inset & selects a \code{.mplstyle} file \\
    \code{font\_scale} & float & multiplies the profile font sizes \\
    \code{label\_style} & \code{()} \code{[]} \code{/} (empty) & axis-label delimiter \\
    \code{delta\_a\_units} & \code{mOD} / \code{x1E3} & \dA{} label convention \\
    \code{cmap} & string & default contour colourmap \\
    \code{time\_axis\_scale} & symlog / log / lin & default time-axis scale \\
    \code{freq\_label} & Omega\_n / Omega\_PP / \dots & 2-D frequency-axis convention \\
    \code{x\_axis\_unit} & native / nm / cm-1 / eV / THz & spectral X-axis display unit \\
    \code{secondary\_axis} & bool & complementary X axis (top) \\
    \code{chirp\_boundary\_handling} & crop / extrapolate & boundary handling for chirp correction \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Fields of the \code{Settings} object.}
  \label{tab:settings}
\end{table}

The \code{label\_style} field controls the delimiter so that an axis reads, for
example, ``Wavelength (nm)'', ``Wavelength [nm]'' or ``Wavelength / nm''. The
\code{delta\_a\_units} field switches the signal-axis label between
``\dA{} / m\,OD'' and ``\dA{} $\times 10^{3}$''; because $1\,\mathrm{mOD}
\equiv \dA \times 10^{3}$, this is a labelling choice only and never rescales
the stored data. The active \code{label\_style} delimiter is honoured for the
m\,OD label on every axis, including the contour colorbar (so ``/'' yields
``\dA{} / m\,OD''); the $\times 10^{3}$ form is always shown without a delimiter.

The style profiles wrap the existing hand-tuned \code{.mplstyle} files rather
than replacing them; \code{font\_scale} is applied as a light multiplicative
overlay on top. Text is always rendered with matplotlib's mathtext engine; the
LaTeX backend (\code{text.usetex}) is never enabled.

The \code{x\_axis\_unit} field converts the spectral (probe) X axis of the transient-spectra and contour plots between wavelength (nm), wavenumber (cm$^{-1}$), energy (eV) and frequency (THz); \code{native} keeps each dataset's stored unit. The conversion is physical (the axis values are recomputed, pivoting through the wavelength), not a relabelling. When \code{secondary\_axis} is enabled a second X axis is drawn on top in the \emph{complementary} unit: cm$^{-1}\leftrightarrow$nm, with nm used as the complement for eV and THz. Both fields default to the active settings and can be overridden per call, e.g. \code{data.plot\_spectra([1, 5, 20], x\_axis\_unit="nm", secondary\_axis=True)}. The earlier \code{dualScale} keyword is retained as a deprecated alias (a truthy value maps to \code{secondary\_axis=True}). Whenever a wavenumber axis (main or secondary) exceeds $5000\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ its tick labels are divided by $1000$ and the axis is relabelled ``Wavenumber ($\times 10^{3}$ cm$^{-1}$)'', so UV/Vis wavenumbers stay legible; IR ranges are left unscaled. The axis data, stored limits and GUI spin boxes remain in true cm$^{-1}$ -- only the displayed tick labels and unit are scaled.

The \code{chirp\_boundary\_handling} field controls how out-of-bounds delays are treated during the chirp correction interpolation step, selecting between \code{crop} and \code{extrapolate} (see \S\ref{sec:chirp}).

Settings are read from and written to a TOML file, with comments preserved on
write so the file remains hand-editable after the GUI saves it:

\begin{lstlisting}
pm.load_settings("settings.toml")   # make the file's settings active
pm.update_settings(profile="poster", label_style="/")
pm.apply_style()                    # apply the profile to matplotlib
pm.save_settings("settings.toml")   # persist (keeps comments)
\end{lstlisting}

A module-level \emph{active} settings object is read by the plotting methods.
Temporary overrides can be scoped with a context manager:

\begin{lstlisting}
with pm.use_settings(delta_a_units="mOD"):
    data.plot_spectra([1, 5, 20])   # this figure only
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{Dataset Stitching: Wavelength and Timescale}\label{sec:oneD:stitching}

Ultrafast experiments often collect transient spectra across distinct spectral windows (such as UV/Vis and NIR spectrometers) or combine ultrafast optical delay lines with electronically synchronised sub-nanosecond to microsecond delays. PyMORGAN groups these operations under the \textbf{Spectra $\to$ Stitch Datasets} menu, providing two dedicated graphical interfaces.

\paragraph{Wavelength stitching (\code{Stitch Wavelength Windows...}).}
When measurements are conducted in adjacent or overlapping wavelength ranges (for instance, a UV/Vis window spanning $350$--$700\,\mathrm{nm}$ and an NIR window spanning $650$--$1400\,\mathrm{nm}$):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Overlap detection:} The tool automatically discovers the shared probe interval $[\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{\mathrm{ovl}}, \lambda_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{ovl}}]$ between Dataset~1 and Dataset~2.
  \item \textbf{Amplitude scaling:} To compensate for slight differences in probe intensity or detector sensitivity, an optimal least-squares scaling coefficient $\alpha$ is evaluated across the shared delay and wavelength grid:
    \begin{equation}
      \alpha = \frac{\sum_{\tau, \lambda} Z_1(\tau, \lambda)\, Z_2(\tau, \lambda)}{\sum_{\tau, \lambda} Z_2(\tau, \lambda)^2}.
    \end{equation}
    The user can choose to scale Dataset~2 to Dataset~1, scale Dataset~1 to Dataset~2, or maintain native unscaled amplitudes.
  \item \textbf{Smooth cosine taper blending:} Across the transition window, signals are seamlessly blended using a raised cosine taper weight:
    \begin{equation}
      w(\lambda) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ 1 + \cos\left( \pi \frac{\lambda - \lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{\mathrm{ovl}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}^{\mathrm{ovl}} - \lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{\mathrm{ovl}}} \right) \right],
    \end{equation}
    \begin{equation}
      Z_{\mathrm{stitched}}(\tau, \lambda) = w(\lambda)\, Z_1(\tau, \lambda) + \big(1 - w(\lambda)\big)\, \alpha Z_2(\tau, \lambda).
    \end{equation}
    This prevents sharp steps or derivative artefacts in the stitched data.
  \item \textbf{Live inspection and export:} The dialog provides an interactive 3-panel display (Dataset~1, Dataset~2, and Stitched preview) with kinetic and spectral cut comparisons before updating the main window or exporting to a new \code{.pdat} file.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Timescale stitching (\code{Stitch Timescales...}).}
When merging femtosecond/picosecond optical delay data with nanosecond/microsecond flash-photolysis or electronic delay measurements:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Delay offset calibration:} Compensates for small timing differences $\Delta \tau_0$ between the independent delay mechanisms so that both axes share an identical time-zero reference.
  \item \textbf{Overlap interpolation and cross-scaling:} Identifies the overlapping delay window (typically $1$--$5\,\mathrm{ns}$), estimates the relative signal scaling factor via linear regression over the overlapping delays, and resamples the dataset onto a unified temporal grid.
  \item The combined time axis preserves fine sub-picosecond temporal resolution around time zero while transitioning smoothly to logarithmic or extended linear steps at late nanosecond and microsecond delays.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)}\label{sec:oneD:svd}

Accessed via \textbf{Analysis $\to$ Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)...}, this tool provides model-free factorisation of the two-dimensional transient matrix $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\tau \times N_\lambda}$:
\begin{equation}
  Z = U \Sigma V^{\mathsf{T}} = \sum_{k=1}^{\min(N_\tau, N_\lambda)} \sigma_k\, \mathbf{u}_k\, \mathbf{v}_k^{\mathsf{T}},
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Sigma = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \dots)$ contains the singular values in descending order, representing the variance/energy carried by each component.
  \item The columns of $U$ ($\mathbf{u}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\tau}$) represent the orthonormal left singular vectors, corresponding to the time-dependent kinetic evolution of each component.
  \item The rows of $V^{\mathsf{T}}$ ($\mathbf{v}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{N_\lambda}$) represent the orthonormal right singular vectors, corresponding to the spectral basis shapes of each component.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Diagnostic scree plot.}
The dialog displays a scree plot showing both the individual singular values $\sigma_k$ (log-scaled) and the cumulative variance explained $\sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i^2 / \sum \sigma_j^2 \times 100\%$. The transition from rapidly falling singular values to a flat, noisy plateau indicates the number of genuine photophysical species present in the sample, distinguishing genuine chemical states from random detector noise.

\paragraph{Low-rank noise filtering and reconstruction.}
Users can select a rank cutoff $K$ (e.g.\ $K=3$) to reconstruct a low-rank, noise-filtered approximation of the transient surface:
\begin{equation}
  Z_{\mathrm{filtered}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sigma_k\, \mathbf{u}_k\, \mathbf{v}_k^{\mathsf{T}}.
\end{equation}
The dialog provides real-time contour previews of the raw data, the low-rank reconstruction, and the residual matrix $Z - Z_{\mathrm{filtered}}$. The filtered dataset can be committed directly to the main PyMORGAN session or exported to disk as a clean \code{.pdat} file.

\subsubsection{Polarization Anisotropy and Magic Angle}\label{sec:oneD:anisotropy}

Time-resolved polarization anisotropy allows isolating transition-dipole orientational relaxation (rotational diffusion) from isotropic population decay. The unified polarization suite is accessed through the top-level \textbf{Anisotropy} menu (as well as \textbf{Spectra $\to$ Calculate polarization anisotropy...}). It seamlessly handles:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PyMESS slow-modulation datasets (\code{.h5}):} Polarization states (parallel and perpendicular) acquired with slow modulation are automatically recognised from metadata, labeled, and paired.
  \item \textbf{Multi-detector instruments:} Hardware setups recording parallel and perpendicular signals on distinct detector channels.
  \item \textbf{Separate files:} Two distinct single-channel measurements (one parallel, one perpendicular) paired directly via the interactive file browser.
  \item \textbf{Legacy MESS folder archives:} Backwards-compatible presets (UV/IR anisotropy and calculated/experimental magic angles) accessible via the legacy submenu.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{G-factor correction.}
To account for differences in spectrometer throughput, grating efficiency, and detector sensitivity for parallel versus perpendicular polarizations, a correction factor $G$ is applied:
\begin{equation}
  G = \frac{\Delta A_\parallel(\tau_{\mathrm{iso}}, \lambda)}{\Delta A_\perp(\tau_{\mathrm{iso}}, \lambda)},
\end{equation}
which can either be specified as a calibrated constant, read directly from acquisition metadata (e.g.\ in PyMESS datasets), or determined by tail-matching at late delay times where rotational diffusion has fully randomised the orientation distribution ($r \to 0$).

\paragraph{Anisotropy and isotropic decay.}
The time-resolved anisotropy $r(\tau, \lambda)$, the isotropic (magic-angle equivalent) absorbance change $\Delta A_{\mathrm{iso}}(\tau, \lambda)$, and the polarization difference $\Delta I(\tau, \lambda)$ are computed according to:
\begin{equation}
  r(\tau, \lambda) = \frac{\Delta A_\parallel(\tau, \lambda) - G\, \Delta A_\perp(\tau, \lambda)}{\Delta A_\parallel(\tau, \lambda) + 2G\, \Delta A_\perp(\tau, \lambda)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A_{\mathrm{iso}}(\tau, \lambda) = \frac{\Delta A_\parallel(\tau, \lambda) + 2G\, \Delta A_\perp(\tau, \lambda)}{3},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \Delta I(\tau, \lambda) = \Delta A_\parallel(\tau, \lambda) - G\, \Delta A_\perp(\tau, \lambda).
\end{equation}
The calculated $\Delta A_{\mathrm{iso}}$ is completely devoid of rotational reorientation dynamics, matching what would be measured directly at the magic angle ($54.7^\circ$). In addition to the rich interactive dialog—featuring simultaneous intensity and $r(\tau)$ kinetic traces, denominator noise thresholding, and probe wavelength slicing—the \textbf{Anisotropy} menu provides instant 1-click execution to replace the active workspace dataset with $r(\tau,\lambda)$, $\Delta A_{\mathrm{iso}}$, or the polarized components for immediate downstream kinetic analysis.

\subsubsection{Time Derivative of Spectra ($\mathrm{d}(\Delta A)/\mathrm{d}t$)}\label{sec:oneD:timederiv}

Accessed via \textbf{Pre-Processing $\to$ Time Derivative of Spectra\ldots} (\code{time\_derivative\_dialog.py}) or programmatically through \code{pymorgan.oneD.process.time\_derivative}, this tool computes the numerical derivative $\partial \Delta A(\tau, \lambda) / \partial \tau$ along the delay axis to accentuate rapid kinetic inflection points and coherent oscillations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Early-delay cutoff:} Optionally trims delays $\tau < \tau_{\min}$ prior to differentiation so that the intense time-zero coherent artefact does not dominate the derivative scale.
  \item \textbf{Time-domain interpolation:} Optionally resamples the signal onto a uniform or denser temporal grid ($N_{\text{interp}}$ points) before differentiation.
  \item \textbf{Pre-smoothing:} Supports Savitzky--Golay polynomial filtering or moving-average smoothing along the delay dimension, with a live interactive preview comparing raw and differentiated kinetic traces at selected probe wavelengths.
\end{enumerate}
